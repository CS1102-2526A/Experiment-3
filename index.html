However, you can also test the pH value of anything you like by making a pH indicator yourself.


How to DIY a pH Indicator?

Anthocyanins are a natural pH indicator because they change colour in response to chemical changes in various substances.

Anthocyanins can be extracted from a wide range of plants, including flowers, fruits, and vegetables. While different plants have varying pH sensitivities, red cabbage is considered the most suitable for use as a pH indicator due to its presentation of a variety of colours across the pH scale, making it easy to observe chemical changes.

When red cabbage is used as an indicator, red cabbage juice, whose original colour is purple-blue, changes from red to yellow and then to green as the pH value increases from 2 to 12.

(Source: Compound Interest https://www.compoundchem.com/2017/05/18/red-cabbage/)


How to Make a pH Indicator with Red Cabbage?

Now, we can observe a neutralization reaction of HCl and NaOH with Universal Indicator by a simple animation.

Step 1: Chop the red cabbage into small pieces.

Step 2: Prepare boiling water and place the red cabbage pieces into it.

Step 3: Wait for 30 minutes until the water becomes a purple-blue colour, and filter the red cabbage pieces.

That purple-blue liquid is your pH indicator!


Simulation - Determining the pH of a Strong-Acid Weak-Base Solution

In this experiment, we will use red cabbage water as our pH indicator.

Materials we needed:

  • Red Cabbage Water
  • Lemon Juice
  • Baking Soda Water

Step 1: Add baking soda water (pH 9) to the red cabbage water. The colour will turn blue-green because the baking soda water is an alkaline liquid.

Step 2: Add the same amount of lemon juice (pH 2) to the same solution. You will observe that the colour changes rapidly. It flashes red for a second, then turns purple-blue, and finally becomes pink.


In Step 2, the red cabbage water turns red because lemon juice is a strong acid. When red cabbage water meets with an acidic liquid, its colour changes from purple-blue to red. Furthermore, since the red cabbage water has already been added to the baking soda water, the combination of an acidic liquid with an alkaline liquid causes neutralization.

This reaction causes the red cabbage water to turn purple-blue for a second. Finally, because lemon juice is a strong acid and baking soda water is a weak base, the final mixture becomes a slightly acidic liquid (~pH 4 - 6). Therefore, the red cabbage water turns pink in the end.
